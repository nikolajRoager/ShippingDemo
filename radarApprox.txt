True radar horizon

Let a be the line from observer to target
let b=Re+h_o
let c=Re+h_t

Let d=Re be the line from observer to target, by definition of radar horizon it is perpendicular to a, so

a = sqrt( (Re+h_o)^2-Re^2 )+sqrt((Re+h_t)^2-Re^2) =
sqrt( Re^2+h_o^2+2Reh_o-Re^2 )+sqrt(Re^2+h_t^2+2Reh_t-Re^2)=
Re (sqrt( (h_o/Re)^2+2h_o/Re )+sqrt( (h_t/Re)^2+2h_t/Re ))

If we drop higher order terms right now we get our "rule-of-thump" approximation back

a = sqrt(2 Re) (sqrt( h_o )+sqrt( h_t ))

Everything is in meters, if we multiply with [nmi]/[m] we get the numerical constant

a= 1.931808187904786 *(sqrt(h_o)+sqrt(h_t)) where a is in nmi, and the heights are in m.

For small distances compared to the radius of the earth, this IS the radar horizon, and that is what we will be using in-game.

I we want to find the true range along the surface, that is Re*A, where A is the angle opposite line a, from the sine relations

a^2=c^2+b^2-2*c*b*cos(A) or put another way

A = cos^-1((-a^2+c^2+b^2)/(2*c*b))

So we can get our answer R_horizon= Re*A

But that requires an inverse cosine, which is expensive
